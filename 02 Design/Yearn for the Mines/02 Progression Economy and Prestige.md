# Yearn for the Mines — Progression, Economy, and Prestige

## Purpose

Expand the incremental and prestige ideas from the source blast into testable routes. This note separates run-level progress, permanent progress, and narrative discovery.

## Core economy layers

### 1. Immediate finds

Loose ore, scrap, artifacts, and saleable objects are the short-cycle reward. They should arrive often enough to make each excavation action legible.

### 2. Spendable mining money

Money buys tools, capacity, workbench improvements, safety supplies, and possibly access fees. It should make the next material tier achievable without becoming a mindless “wait longer” tax.

### 3. Permanent tokens

A collapse, completed descent, or major discovery can award a separate token. Tokens unlock slight persistent buffs or new starting options across attempts.

### 4. Discovery record

Lore, collectible objects, strata records, and temple evidence should be recorded separately from money. This prevents the player from needing to sell every meaningful discovery and supports a completionist route.

## Hands-first opening

Starting with hands is stronger than starting with a bucket if the opening is short and authored around that limitation.

### Why it works

- communicates helplessness and physical labor immediately;
- gives Callous a clear early relevance;
- makes the first purchased tool feel like a transformation;
- creates a before/after comparison for the first upgrade;
- supports a tactile first-person opening.

### Risk

If hand excavation is slow, repetitive, or visually unclear, the player may quit before the incremental loop begins. The opening needs fast feedback, generous first rewards, and a near-term first purchase.

## Tool purchase versus tool discovery

### Purchase route

The player must earn and spend money to unlock the next tool.

**Benefits:** reinforces the incremental market loop, makes selling meaningful, and gives the player a clear target.

**Risks:** can feel artificial if tools are only shop menus; may create a grind wall.

### Discovery route

The player finds tools inside the mine.

**Benefits:** exploration feels materially rewarding; tool finds can become story moments.

**Risks:** can undermine the economy and make tool progression luck-dependent.

### Hybrid route

**Working recommendation:** purchases should unlock the standard capability ladder, while rare found tools, blueprints, or damaged equipment provide discounts, alternate versions, or new side-grades. This preserves the market loop without making exploration feel financially irrelevant.

## Proposed upgrade families

### Callous

User-supplied side idea: reduces energy consumed by labor actions.

Potential versions:

- lower energy cost for hand excavation;
- slower energy drain while holding an excavation action;
- improved recovery after stopping work;
- less penalty from repeated manual labor;
- eventually, a threshold that lets the player work exhausted but slower.

**Design risk:** if energy is only a timer, Callous may become mandatory rather than interesting. It should compete with capacity, speed, safety, and find-quality upgrades.

### Grit

User-supplied side idea: a skill whose intended “unvestable” meaning still needs clarification.

Potential versions to contest:

- delayed payoff: benefits activate after sustained labor without resting;
- commitment: once selected, it cannot be removed during a descent;
- recovery: increases output or survival when energy is low;
- risk tolerance: reduces collapse penalties but increases danger exposure;
- persistence: preserves one layer of mine progress after a collapse.

Do not choose a meaning silently. See [[02 Design/Ideas/Side Notes - Callous and Grit]].

### Other support families

- **Grip:** faster or more reliable hand/tool interactions.
- **Breath:** energy recovery and work-rest rhythm.
- **Pack/Load:** carry capacity and deposit frequency.
- **Surveying:** reveals likely ore or collectible locations.
- **Shoring:** increases collapse warning time or stabilizes a route.
- **Sorting:** improves sift quality or rare-find chance.
- **Light:** increases visibility and reveals markings.
- **Debt/credit:** allows earlier tool purchases at a future cost.

These are proposals, not a required launch tree.

## Prestige routes

### Route A — Reset after collapse

The mine collapses at a depth or risk threshold. The player returns to a fresh attempt and receives tokens based on depth, finds, and discoveries.

**Strong for:** repeated short runs and a clear fail/reset loop.

**Risk:** collapse can feel like punishment if the player has no agency over when it happens.

### Route B — Voluntary retirement

The player can return to the surface and bank a token multiplier before the mine becomes unsafe.

**Strong for:** strategic incremental play and player-controlled pacing.

**Risk:** optimal play may become repetitive shallow banking.

### Route C — Multiple mine phases

Each mine phase ends in a collapse, but the next phase retains permanent infrastructure and opens a new depth layer.

**Strong for:** authored story progression and controlled scope.

**Risk:** needs clear differentiation so it does not feel like the same tunnel recolored.

### Working recommendation

Prototype Route C with a voluntary-retirement option inside each phase. A collapse should be a dramatic transition, not merely a reload screen. Tokens should unlock slight buffs, tools, starting conveniences, or new options—not make the base game unplayable without grinding.

## Anti-exploit review

Watch for:

- selling and re-buying loops that create money;
- saving after a sale but before an item removal;
- collapse rewards being granted repeatedly on reload;
- shallow mine routes outperforming risky deep routes;
- idle/automation upgrades making manual play irrelevant too early;
- energy reductions reaching zero cost;
- capacity upgrades multiplying rare-find value without corresponding risk;
- prestige bonuses stacking into an unavoidable optimal route;
- temple collectibles being sold and still counted as discovered;
- partial tool purchases surviving a failed transaction.

## First economy test

The smallest test should answer:

1. Is hand labor satisfying for 30–90 seconds?
2. Does the player understand what the first purchase enables?
3. Does selling feel like a meaningful choice or a mandatory menu chore?
4. Does the new tool create a visible new material boundary?
5. Does the player want to continue one layer deeper?
