# Yearn for the Mines — Lore, Child Labor, and Endings

## Purpose

Develop the secret-lore direction carefully. The source blast proposes hidden child-labor lore, a collapsing mine, and a temple ending. These elements can create a memorable mystery, but they also create tone, ethical, and platform-presentation responsibilities.

This note is not canon. It is a set of routes to contest.

## The central narrative tension

The surface market presents mining as a clean incremental opportunity: work, sell, upgrade, descend. The mine's records reveal that the system was built on people who were treated as replaceable labor.

The strongest version of the mystery should make the player reconsider the economy they have been participating in, not use child labor as a disposable shock joke.

## Possible lore frameworks

### Framework A — Historical record

The mine previously employed children under coercive conditions. Notes, tags, toys, work assignments, and censored ledgers reveal what happened.

**Strength:** direct and understandable.

**Risk:** requires respectful handling and can clash with a light comedy tone.

### Framework B — Exploitative institution

The mine belongs to a company, kingdom, or machine administration that hid child labor behind euphemisms such as “apprenticeship,” “junior extraction,” or “family shifts.”

**Strength:** supports satirical item text, corporate systems, and a critique of incremental extraction.

**Risk:** can become too familiar or preachy without specific character stories.

### Framework C — Supernatural memory

The temple or mine preserves echoes of children who worked there, and the descent reveals memory fragments rather than a literal historical archive.

**Strength:** supports mystery and a more surreal ending.

**Risk:** may soften the real-world subject too much or make the reveal unclear.

### Framework D — Player complicity mystery

The player gradually discovers that the current mining operation is repeating the old system, perhaps through the prestige loop itself.

**Strength:** connects lore directly to mechanics.

**Risk:** demands a clear alternative action or the story becomes accusatory without agency.

## Working narrative recommendation

Start with Framework B plus a restrained version of Framework D:

- the mine was operated by an institution that hid dangerous child labor behind respectable language;
- the current incremental economy is built from the same records and incentives;
- the player's repeated resets slowly reveal that “progress” may be preserving the system rather than escaping it;
- the temple contains evidence, testimony, or a surviving mechanism that gives the player a choice about what to do with the mine.

Do not make the player a secret child-labor participant unless the project explicitly wants that moral framing and can support it.

## Lore delivery plan

Use indirect, cumulative discovery rather than one late exposition dump:

1. altered work labels and suspiciously small uniform sizes;
2. collectible personal objects with names and dates;
3. incomplete shift ledgers and censored safety reports;
4. graffiti, counting marks, or messages in hard-to-reach spaces;
5. abandoned classrooms, dormitory traces, or tool-sizing evidence;
6. named fragments of testimony in the journal;
7. a final document or temple mechanism that recontextualizes the run loop.

The game should not require the player to find every dark clue to understand the main progression.

## The two endings

### Ending 1 — The mine collapses

The player pushes too far, loses the route, or chooses extraction over preservation. The collapse can be:

- a failure ending;
- a bittersweet escape with recovered wealth;
- a cycle ending where the next run begins with the same system;
- a false ending that only becomes meaningful after enough evidence is collected.

### Ending 2 — The temple is found

The player reaches a buried temple or pre-existing chamber. It should not be only a treasure room. Possible functions:

- a memorial and record of the people who came before;
- the original source of the mine's wealth;
- a machine that can stabilize or erase the mine;
- a place where the player can end the extraction cycle;
- a deeper mystery that opens a second campaign/phase.

### Contestation question

Should the temple be a reward for efficiency, a reward for exploration, or a moral alternative to maximum extraction? The answer determines whether the ending reinforces the incremental economy or challenges it.

## Tone guardrails

- Do not turn child labor into a collectible joke.
- Avoid graphic depictions unless explicitly justified and handled with care.
- Let the humor target the mining institution, euphemisms, corporate greed, or absurd paperwork—not the children.
- Use warning/age-rating and store-presentation review before public marketing leans on the subject.
- Keep the player’s emotional response earned through specific evidence and individual traces.

## Open lore questions

- Is the mine historical, contemporary, fantasy, or mechanically surreal?
- Who owns the mine now?
- Why does the player keep returning after collapse?
- Is the player trying to get rich, rescue someone, expose the truth, or simply survive?
- Does the temple end extraction, reveal a larger system, or offer a third choice?
- Is the child-labor history optional background, central mystery, or the main emotional spine?
