## First evidence targets

### 1. Hands-first labor feel

Can the player understand and enjoy the first 30–90 seconds of manual excavation without feeling that the game is withholding the real game?

### 2. Sifting reward feel

Does sifting create anticipation and discovery, or does it feel like an unnecessary animation between clicks?

### 3. First purchase breakpoint

Does buying the first tool feel like a meaningful capability change that opens a visible new material tier?

### 4. Incremental pacing

Does the next purchase arrive quickly enough to establish momentum while still making money meaningful?

### 5. First-person comfort

Can the player work material comfortably without camera fatigue, poor target selection, or unclear interaction range?

### 6. Collapse pressure

Does collapse create a meaningful risk/reward decision, or does it feel like an arbitrary reset?

### 7. Lore integration

Do hidden records and objects create curiosity without treating child labor as a cheap collectible surprise?

## Evidence policy

- A prototype result can contest a proposal without needing to invalidate the fantasy.
- A good-feeling interaction does not automatically justify its economy, narrative, or production cost.
- Record negative results as carefully as positive results.
- Separate “the mechanic works” from “the mechanic is fun,” “the mechanic is readable,” and “the mechanic is worth its scope.”
- Update [[04 Contestation and Decision Log]] when evidence changes a claim’s status.
- Update [[06 Tasks/Next Actions]] when evidence creates a concrete next action.
