# Lore and World Plan

## Why this note exists

Define a small, production-friendly lore spine that can support combat, upgrades, item text, arenas, unlocks, and Steam-facing presentation without requiring a cinematic campaign.

This is a working framework, not canon. The questionnaire is the authority for the next revision.

## Core lore promise

> A stubborn paper-built mechanic fights through a collapsing machine-world, turning discarded parts, bad ideas, and impossible weapons into one more chance to keep the world from being folded shut.

The world should make the game's production constraints feel intentional:

- flat characters are inhabitants of a layered paper/mechanical reality;
- chunky arenas are built from panels, scraps, toys, and machine parts;
- weapon comedy is normal local culture, not a detached parody layer;
- upgrades are physical modifications, remembered tricks, rumors, or temporary overclock states;
- runs are attempts to reach deeper into the failing machine rather than identical abstract arenas.

## Working setting candidates

### Candidate A — The Folded Machine

The world is a giant paper-mechanical engine. A spreading fold is compressing places, people, and possibilities into unusable shapes. The player repairs the path by defeating hostile maintenance systems and stealing their operating principles.

**Strengths:** directly supports paper physics, arenas, mechanical enemies, and run structure.

**Risks:** could become visually generic if every area is only cardboard and gears.

### Candidate B — The Scrap Crown

A ruling machine-court controls the supply of energy and weapon parts. The player is an obsolete repair unit who keeps finding illegal modifications in the scrap stream.

**Strengths:** supports factions, shops, weapon comedy, and satirical item text.

**Risks:** needs a clear reason for arena combat and repeated runs.

### Candidate C — The Unfinished World

The world is being improvised page by page. Failed sketches, discarded props, and corrected enemies become real threats. The player is a character trying to reach the authoring layer before the last page is removed.

**Strengths:** supports meta-fictional humor and evolving visual styles.

**Risks:** can excuse anything unless rules remain firm.

**Current working recommendation:** use Candidate A as the physical world spine and borrow the faction/comedy utility of Candidate B. Keep Candidate C as a tone-only possibility until the questionnaire clarifies how self-aware the story should be.

## Player role

Working placeholder: **the Ratchet** is a small repair/combat unit built to retrieve, reconnect, or re-route broken machine components. The name can remain a working title until the character identity is reviewed.

The player is not chosen because they are the strongest. They are useful because they can survive awkward interfaces, improvise with rejected tools, and keep moving when the official systems have failed.

## Central conflict

The machine-world's central power system is converting every problem into more rigid control. The result is a world that is becoming clean, efficient, and lifeless. The player fights through its defensive layers to recover the **unfolding engine** or equivalent core mechanism before the world is locked into one dead configuration.

This gives the game a simple dramatic question:

> Do you restore the machine to its old shape, or teach it to survive by becoming stranger?

## Factions and encounter logic

Keep factions small and readable. Each faction should justify an enemy family, visual prop language, or upgrade category.

- **Maintenance Grunts:** routine systems that enforce the current machine rules.
- **Rangers/Surveyors:** keep-away units that map and correct movement through the arena.
- **Wardens:** miniboss-scale systems that protect important transitions.
- **Scrapfolk:** possible neutral/shop/journal characters; do not require combat implementation yet.
- **The Fold Authority:** late-run or boss-level force that wants a single stable shape at any cost.

These are labels, not committed content. A faction becomes real only when it improves enemy readability, item text, arena identity, or progression choices.

## Lore delivery plan

Use low-cost, high-repeatability channels first:

1. weapon names and descriptions;
2. upgrade descriptions that reveal how the world thinks;
3. brief room-intro/outro cards;
4. journal entries for enemies, weapons, and discoveries;
5. hub or between-run conversations if a hub earns its scope;
6. environmental props and arena signage;
7. full scenes only when they answer a player question that smaller channels cannot.

## Tone rules

- Jokes should make the world more specific, not dissolve every consequence.
- The player can be ridiculous while the problem remains sincere.
- Item descriptions should reveal character, history, or a mechanical truth whenever possible.
- Repeated comic words such as `BONK`, `CLANK`, `ZAP`, or `WHAM` should be treated as readable combat feedback first and lore flavor second.
- Avoid parodying a single external franchise; use the project's own paper/mechanical vocabulary.

## Production boundaries

Do not require:

- a fully voiced campaign;
- dozens of named factions;
- a large overworld;
- lore-only cutscenes for every unlock;
- a complex chronology before the first playable slice works.

The first lore pass only needs to answer: who is fighting, what is being threatened, why these arenas are connected, and why upgrades are plausible.

## Open questions

- Is the player's name/role actually **Ratchet**, or should the final character name differ?
- Is the paper world literal, metaphorical, or a presentation layer?
- Does the player want sincere stakes beneath the comedy?
- What is the machine's original purpose?
- What does a successful run change in the world?
- Are Scrapfolk allies, rivals, merchants, or only journal voices?
- Does the player repair the world, escape it, or deliberately remake it?

## Canon policy

Until the questionnaire is reviewed, treat this note as a lore seed. New lore should be added as a candidate, linked to a system or presentation need, and marked `working`, `open`, or `canon` rather than silently becoming canon.
